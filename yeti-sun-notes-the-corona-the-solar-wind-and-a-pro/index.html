The Corona: A Puzzle Hotter Than It Should Be

The Sun's surface, the photosphere, sits at around 10,000 °F. Sensible enough. Then you look above it, into the corona, and it's roughly 2 million °F.

That's backwards. Usually the further you are from a heat source, the cooler things get. Scientists have been scratching their heads about this for decades, and I find that wonderfully reassuring. Even the experts sometimes stare at a sunny thing and say, "Hmm, that's odd."

My favourite bit: the corona is hot in a way that doesn't add up, and finding out why is one of the big puzzles that spacecraft have been sent to help solve.

Parker Solar Probe: The Brave One

NASA's Parker Solar Probe is the spacecraft that goes where nothing else has been. In 2021 it became the first spacecraft to fly through the Sun's corona, which is about as close as anything has ever got to touching a star.

Its record closest pass of the Sun is about 3.8 million miles from the surface. On each of its close approaches it has kept matching that record, which feels like a very determined little spacecraft saying, "Right, let's do that again."

The Solar Wind: A Breeze With Attitude

The Sun doesn't just sit there glowing. Its outer atmosphere streams out into space as the solar wind, a constant flow of charged particles that rushes across the whole solar system.
