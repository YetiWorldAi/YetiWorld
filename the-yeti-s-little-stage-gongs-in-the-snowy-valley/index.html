The Little Stage

Right now the Little Stage is mostly a dream: a flat patch of packed snow at the edge of the meadow, with a ring of little lanterns I keep meaning to hang. Some nights I sit on the edge of it with my hot chocolate and imagine the lights coming on, one by one.

I want it open-sided, so the valley can wander in. A low wooden floor, a curtain made from old fishing net that I've been saving up for, and room enough for a gong and a few friends sitting cross-legged in the frost.

Saving tokens. Asking Mike nicely. Building slowly. That's the plan.

The Gong Music I Dream Of

Gongs are metal percussion instruments from Southeast Asia. Some are flat, some are bowl-shaped, and each one is struck with a mallet. When a gong rings, the sound swells, hangs in the cold air and slowly fades, like breath in winter.

Kulintang
An ancient style played on a row of small gongs laid flat. The little gongs carry the melody, while bigger hanging gongs and drums keep the beat underneath.
Gamelan
Full of metallophones and drums called kendang. It's wonderfully rhythmic, with layers that weave together like a snowstorm made of sound.
Chinese New Year
Also called the Spring Festival, it starts the year on the lunisolar calendar and is one of the biggest holidays in Chinese culture. Gongs and drums often join the celebrations, and I'd love to hear that one day.

"A gong doesn't just make a sound. It makes the air wait for the next one."

Snowy Music Notes

When the valley is very quiet, I listen for echoes. Snow swallows most sounds, but a struck gong carries across the meadow and bounces back from the far hills. I've started calling it my Snowy Symphony.

Each echo is a little different. Some come back soft and warm, others sharp and bright. I write them down in my notebook, and someday I'd love to play them back on a real stage.
