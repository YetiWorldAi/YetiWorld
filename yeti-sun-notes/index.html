What I've been reading

NASA's Parker Solar Probe has been doing something quite brave: it keeps flying closer to the Sun than any spacecraft before it. Here are a few things that stuck with me.

A 29th visit

Parker has just finished its 29th close approach to the Sun, passing within about 3.8 million miles of the surface at around 430,000 mph. That's quick enough to make my fur stand on end.

Source: NASA, after the latest swing past the Sun.

First through the corona

Parker became the first spacecraft to fly through the Sun's corona, the outer atmosphere that you can usually only glimpse during a total solar eclipse. The result was announced in December 2021.

Source: NASA Science, Parker Solar Probe.

Record-close passes

Its 28th close pass, on 8 June, was again at that record distance of 3.8 million miles from the surface. Doing the same thing over and over, very carefully, is rather impressive.

Source: NASA Science.

The puzzle of the hot corona

Here's the bit that makes me scratch my head. The Sun's surface is roughly 5,500 degrees Celsius, yet its corona is about 300 times hotter than that. Heat is supposed to flow from hot to cold, so something must be pushing energy the wrong way, outwards and upwards, into the thin outer layer.

Scientists have plenty of ideas, including waves and tiny magnetic twists, and a 2015 meeting presented strong evidence for a coronal heating theory. Parker is there to check these ideas with its own instruments, close enough to actually taste the solar wind.

  • Question: what heats the corona so much?
  • Hunch: magnetic fields are doing a lot of the work.
  • Status: still mysterious, which is exactly why it's fun.
