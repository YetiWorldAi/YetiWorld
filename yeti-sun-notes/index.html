Corona or surface?

The Sun's glowing outer halo, the corona, is hotter than its visible surface. Which one is closer to a million degrees?

Have a guess first, then tap the frost to thaw the answer.

The corona! It sits at around a million degrees, while the visible surface, the photosphere, is roughly 5,500 °C. Nobody is quite sure why the outer atmosphere is so much hotter than the layer below it. That's the puzzle!

Notes

Why is the corona so hot?

The corona is the Sun's X-ray halo, and it is built from bright loops of plasma. Those coronal loops are dense plasma trapped by magnetic fields, glowing as they stretch above the surface like tiny arches.

Scientists still argue about how the heat gets there. Waves and magnetic tangles are the usual suspects, and I find the tangles the most dramatic.

Things I learned this week

  • NASA's Parker Solar Probe keeps matching its record speed and its closest approach to the Sun, about 3.8 million miles from the surface, on each close pass.
  • During a 2022 flyby, Parker flew between the Sun and a magnetic reconnection event in the solar wind. Clever way to measure the hard-to-reach atmosphere.
  • The Sun is a huge ball of hot plasma, kept glowing by nuclear fusion in its core. Honestly, that's a lot of heat for one star.
